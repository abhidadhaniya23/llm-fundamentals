# Review Lenses

Use these lenses in step 4 of the workflow. Recommend the 3 "Always" lenses plus at most 2 "When relevant" lenses that fit the initiative type and idea; the user may add more. Each review follows the same pattern: gap table ranked High / Medium / Low → ask only the High items (at most 2 batches of 3 questions) → Medium / Low items go to "Open Questions" with the recommended default → update only after confirmation → log rejected items in "Decisions & Exclusions".

Every question carries a priority tag. A lens may be marked "Not applicable" as a whole.

## Always

### 1. Scope & Stakeholders
- Are all user and stakeholder groups named, including indirect ones (administrators, auditors, support, external users)? (High)
- Are the sponsor, decision-maker and owner named? (High)
- Does every goal have at least one must-have that delivers it? (High)
- What is explicitly out of scope? (High)
- Are the core user journeys covered end to end (start → main use → finish / exit)? (Medium)
- Does each goal have a success measure and an owner? (Medium)

### 2. Value & Risks
- What are the expected benefits, and for whom? (High)
- Which options were considered, including "do nothing" and build / buy / extend? (High)
- What are the top risks and their impact? (High)
- What are the rough effort, cost or budget, if known? (Medium)
- Which assumptions are riskiest, and how can they be validated? (Medium)
- Adoption & change: who must work differently, what training and communication are needed? (High for process / operating change and organisational initiatives, otherwise Medium)

### 3. Constraints
- Are there fixed deadlines or dates? (High)
- Does the initiative handle sensitive or personal data? (High)
- Which regulations or corporate policies apply, if any? (High)
- Are technologies mandated or excluded, or are there data location requirements? (Medium)
- Is there a budget cap? (Medium)
- Rule: mark every regulation *(to be validated)* by Legal / Compliance.

## When relevant

### 4. Benchmarks & Alternatives
Use when the user names a benchmark, or when an established category of products, services or practices exists.
- Ask the user to name the reference product(s), service(s) or practice(s), or propose 2–3 and let the user pick or skip. (High)
- Research the public capabilities of the reference(s). (Medium)
- List capabilities the reference offers that the vision lacks; mark which are core vs. optional. (Medium)
- Ask for the build / buy / extend position and the reasons. (High)

### 5. Market & Competitors
Use for initiatives aimed at external customers or a competitive market.
- Target segments and their size *(to be validated)*. (Medium)
- Main alternatives customers use today, and the differentiation. (High)
- Pricing or value proposition assumptions. (Medium)

### 6. Commercial & Operating Model
Use when cost allocation, pricing, multiple business units or a support organisation matter.
- Charging or pricing model. (Medium)
- Support model and service levels. (Medium)
- Sharing across organisational units. (Low)

### 7. Integrations & Data
Use when the initiative depends on other systems, processes or data flows.
- Which existing systems, processes or teams does it touch? (High)
- What does it replace, and when does that retire? (Medium)
- Data ownership and migration. (Medium)

### 8. Data & AI
Use when the initiative uses analytics, machine learning or generative AI.
- Which decisions or tasks AI supports, and with what human oversight. (High)
- Data used for AI and its quality, rights and privacy. (High)
- Transparency, accuracy and fallback when AI is wrong. (Medium)

### 9. Regulations & Compliance (deep dive)
Use for regulated areas.
- Which regions and industries apply? (High)
- Which laws and policies apply? (High)
- Must the initiative be configurable for future rules? (Medium)

Technical quality (hosting, access, security controls, scalability) belongs to the architecture, not the vision. Capture only non-negotiables as Known Constraints.
