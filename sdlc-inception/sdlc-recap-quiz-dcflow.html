SDLC: From Idea to Done - Recap (DC Flow)

Running example: the Demand Assistant. Try to answer each question first, then click it to see the answer.

1. What is SDLC

1. What does SDLC stand for, and what is its goal?

Software Development Life Cycle: a structured process to plan, build, test, deliver and maintain software. The goal is the right product, built right, delivered predictably.

2. Name the seven SDLC phases in order.

Requirements, Analysis, Design, Implementation, Testing, Deployment, Maintenance.

3. Apply three phases to DC Flow: what is the example and the artifact for Requirements, Design and Testing?
  • Requirements: Sabine's demand must reach triage without weeks of chasing. Artifact: vision / epic
  • Design: coordinator, six agents, tools, reviewer, approval service. Artifact: architecture diagram
  • Testing: missing / vague / not stated / complete cases. Artifact: test cases, reports
4. Who are the two personas used all day, and what does each one drive?
  • Sabine (Finance Ops, Business Partner): the user we build for. She drives the value: vision, epics, stories.
  • The approvers (Demand Owner, Architect / TPO, Manager): the reason for the gates. They drive the controls: evidence, gates, audit trail.
5. What is the governing rule of DC Flow?

AI prepares the evidence, people make the decisions. Nothing leaves LoDA until a person approves it.

2. Waterfall vs Agile

6. How does Waterfall work, and when does it fit?

Phases run in sequence, and each one is signed off before the next starts. Change after sign-off is a change request. It fits stable, fixed, often regulated scope.

7. For DC Flow, when does first user feedback arrive under Waterfall and under Agile?

Waterfall: around week 28, after about 30 weeks in total. Agile: around week 4. Both figures are illustrative.

8. Name the four Agile Manifesto values.
  • Individuals and interactions over processes and tools
  • Working software over comprehensive documentation
  • Customer collaboration over contract negotiation
  • Responding to change over following a plan

"Over" does not mean "instead of".

9. What is the V-Model, and where is it still common?

Waterfall with a test phase mirrored to each design phase. It is still common in regulated and automotive contexts.

10. Where does the demand owner see the Demand Assistant under Agile?

At the sprint review, every two weeks, instead of after about seven months.

3. Agile end to end

11. Put the Agile flow in order, from Inception to Done.

Inception, Epic Map, High-level Estimation, Release Plan (Milestones), Epics, Stories, Sprint Planning, Plan, Code, Review, Test, Done.

12. What is the difference between an output and an outcome? Give a DC Flow example.

Output: "the assistant is deployed". Outcome: "LoDA-held time per demand drops from 25-30 days to 5". Inception focuses on outcomes.

13. What is the problem DC Flow solves, in numbers?

A demand takes two to three months before development starts, and about 25-30 days of that is LoDA's own time: chasing the questionnaire, writing minutes and waiting on approvals. The baseline is estimated and must be validated against JIRA timestamps.

14. Name the five outcomes in the DC Flow vision and their targets.
  • Faster internal handling: LoDA-held time per demand, 25-30 days to 5 days or less
  • Less manual effort: about 30 hrs to 8 hrs or less per demand
  • Fewer questionnaire rounds: about 3 to 1.5 or less
  • More capacity, same team: 10-30 demands a month to 30+
  • Full auditability: steps and closure reasons logged in JIRA, partial to 100%
15. Name three inception risks and the epic each one becomes.
  • Sensitive data reaches an external AI provider: local model only, routed centrally (E2)
  • Owners over-trust the AI recommendation: evidence with sources, human decides (E9)
  • Approver SLAs are not adopted: reminders, escalation, SLA dashboard (E7)
  • Misclassification at intake loses a demand: owner spot-check, low-confidence routing (E3)
  • Poor drafts increase review time: automatic reviewer before a person sees it (E7)
  • Thin past-demand history weakens estimates: curated knowledge base, confidence ranges (E12)

A risk with no epic is a risk nobody owns.

16. What is an epic map and what is it used for?

A visual map of all epics, arranged by capability (left to right) and priority or release (top to bottom). It shows the whole product at a glance and is used to slice releases and milestones horizontally.

17. Which estimation technique is used for epics and which for stories?
  • Epics: T-shirt sizing (XS, S, M, L, XL)
  • Stories: story points with Planning Poker (1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13)

An XL epic is a signal to split it. Story points measure complexity, effort and uncertainty, relative to a reference story.

18. Which two DC Flow epics have Low estimate confidence, and why?

E9 Triage and dual approval, and E11 Commercial package. Both depend on a curated past-demand library that does not exist yet. Flag them as risks and plan a spike early.

4. Epics

19. What is an epic, and where does it sit in the hierarchy?

A large body of work that delivers one clear business outcome. It is too big for one sprint and is broken into stories.

Initiative / Theme, Epic, Story, Task.

20. Why do epics matter?

Alignment, prioritisation, roadmap and funding, traceability (idea to epic to story to commit to test), and scope control.

21. Name the sections of a good epic.

Title, problem / context, outcome and success metrics, users / personas, scope and out of scope, epic acceptance criteria, NFRs, dependencies and risks, size, milestone.

22. What is the worked epic E5, and what is its size and milestone?

E5 Questionnaire and gap detection. Size L, milestone Pilot. Outcome: every gap in a returned questionnaire is flagged at once and asked in a single approved follow-up.

23. In E5, what is out of scope and why?

Approving or sending the follow-up (E7), BRD / DDS checks (E8) and triage (E9). Clear boundaries prevent scope creep.

5. Stories and INVEST

24. What is the user story format, and what are the 3 Cs?

As a persona I want capability so that benefit. The "so that" is the most important part.

The 3 Cs: Card (short description), Conversation (discussion), Confirmation (acceptance criteria).

25. What does INVEST stand for?
  • Independent
  • Negotiable
  • Valuable
  • Estimable
  • Small
  • Testable

If a story fails Estimable, run a spike. Fails Small, split it. Fails Testable, the acceptance criteria are missing.

26. What is the Given / When / Then format used for?

Acceptance criteria. They cover the happy path, edge cases and errors, and become test cases directly.

27. Story S5.3 flags gaps in a returned questionnaire. Name its five acceptance cases.
  • Missing: a field answered "TBD" is flagged as missing with a reason
  • Vague: "before year-end close" is flagged as vague and an exact date is requested
  • Not stated: SAP company codes never mentioned are flagged as not stated
  • Complete: all mandatory fields answered, so no gaps reported and no follow-up drafted
  • Nothing sent: the follow-up is a draft only, and nothing leaves LoDA until the owner approves

Story points: 8.

28. List the six stories of E5 with their points. How many sprints is the total?
  • S5.1 Send the questionnaire pre-filled from the original email: 3
  • S5.2 Merge answers arriving across several replies: 5
  • S5.3 Flag missing, vague and not-stated answers: 8
  • S5.4 Draft one consolidated follow-up for owner approval: 8
  • S5.5 Remind on schedule and escalate after three rounds: 5
  • S5.6 Write every step and the approval to JIRA: 5

Total 34 points. At a velocity of about 20 per sprint that is roughly 2 sprints, which matches the L T-shirt size.

29. At what size should a story be split, and name three splitting patterns.

At 13 points or more. Patterns: by workflow step, by data or type, by business rule, by interface, happy path first, spike.

30. What is the difference between the Definition of Ready and the Definition of Done?

DoR protects developers from vague stories (story format, G/W/T criteria, NFRs, estimated, dependencies known, small enough). DoD protects users from half-done work (reviewed PR merged, criteria pass, tests green, no critical findings, gates pass, docs updated, PO accepted).

6. Dev cycle: Plan, Code, Review

31. What is the gate at each step of Plan, Code, Review and Test?
  • Plan: tech lead approves the plan before code
  • Code: developer steers and checks each phase
  • Review: reviewer approves the PR
  • Test: QA confirms the tests prove the acceptance criteria
32. Why must tests come from the acceptance criteria and not from the code?

Otherwise the AI "tests its own homework": it generates tests that just confirm whatever the code does.

33. Which AI skill is used at each step from idea to review?
  • Idea to Vision: idea-to-vision
  • Vision to Epic: epic-creation
  • Epic to Stories: story-mode
  • Story to Plan: plan-mode
  • Plan to Code: code-generation
  • Code to Review: review-mode

7. Sprints and milestones

34. Name the five sprint ceremonies and their purpose.
  • Sprint Planning: pick stories, commit to a sprint goal
  • Daily Stand-up: sync, surface blockers
  • Backlog Refinement: prepare upcoming stories (DoR)
  • Sprint Review: demo working software, get feedback
  • Retrospective: improve the process
35. What is velocity, and what should you never do with it?

Story points completed per sprint (average of the last 3). It is a planning tool for the team. Never use it as a performance metric or compare it across teams.

36. Name the three DC Flow milestones, their goal and exit criteria.
  • MVP: prove value on replayed demands. Exit: demand registered in JIRA, questionnaire sent.
  • Pilot: one demand owner, real demands. Exit: gaps flagged, nothing sent unapproved.
  • GA: all owners, fully auditable. Exit: commercials live, 100% JIRA completeness.

A milestone is a business checkpoint with exit criteria, not a date on its own.

37. Why do the reviewer and approval service (E7) land before the Pilot?

The Pilot is when the first draft could reach a Business Partner, and nothing may reach one unapproved.

38. Why is so much of the DC Flow epic map platform work?

On a self-hosted stack the connectors, model router, reviewer and approval service have to be built before any functional epic can finish, so they sit in the MVP and early Pilot.

39. What does traceability give you, and why is it also a product requirement for DC Flow?

Every artifact links to the one before it: Idea, Vision, Epic E5, Story S5.3, Plan, Commit/PR, Tests, Done. An auditor can walk back from code to Sabine's problem. For DC Flow every decision must also be traceable to a named approver.

Wrap-up

40. What stays with people, even when AI accelerates every step?

Accountability. A human gate approves at every step.

41. Retro: where did AI help most, where did it get it wrong, and who caught it?

Open question for your team. Typically AI finds consistency issues, while humans find "this isn't what the PO meant".
